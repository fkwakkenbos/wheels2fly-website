The Challenge

Millions live without access to a wheelchair.

The WHO estimates tens of millions of people need a wheelchair but cannot access one. The barriers are consistent — and preventable.

Too Expensive

Standard NGO wheelchairs cost around €395 — unaffordable for most families without external subsidy or international aid.

Hard to Maintain

Repairs require specialist technicians. In remote areas, a broken wheelchair can mean weeks without mobility and independence.

Not Locally Available

Supply chains rarely reach rural communities — where the need is greatest and the distance from aid the furthest.

The Solution

The Polaris
Wheelchair

A fully modular wheelchair platform built for self-assembly, independent maintenance, and local repair. A Poka-Yoke connection system ensures components only go together the right way — no specialist required, no manual needed beyond pictograms.

Our Approach

A new model for global mobility.

01

Design for Local Manufacturing

CNC milling, sheet metal bending — no welding. Producible anywhere with basic industrial capacity.

02

Flat-Pack Distribution

Ships as a compact kit. Up to 3× more wheelchairs per container. Lower costs, faster NGO deployment.

03

Community Assembly

Pictogram-based instructions. No text, no specialists. Assembly creates ownership and enables local repair.

04

Local Production

Long-term: local factories, local jobs, local supply chains. Communities build and own their mobility solutions.

Next Milestone: Field Test in Zambia
15 prototype wheelchairs deployed with BluSpring and local partners — evaluating assembly experience, durability, user satisfaction, and clinical outcomes.
